Bun Tsu

The Art of Dependency

Is your cybersecurity team giving you cryptic requirements that you can’t really understand, but they sound really important? Bun Tsu can help you automate this!

bunx @wisdomousai/bun-tsu

It only reads, and never prints your secrets.

A reading

He looks at your lockfile, your install scripts, your version ranges, the names of your packages and the files where secrets like to hide. Every omen is grounded in something real.

The Scroll of Bun Tsu, concerning doomed-app

Your treasury is the target. Every road in your camp leads to it. And the enemy is already inside.

I.The stranger who calls himself colrs wears the face of your friend colors, but a letter was lost on the road.

II.Your general’s seal lies on the tavern table, in the place called .npmrc.

III.You keep no map of the ground you stand on. When the terrain shifts in the night, you will not know which night it was.

“He who runs bunx on a name he cannot spell has already lost.” Bun Tsu

The plain scroll

I.Critical colrs is one letter from colors, and its postinstall script evals code.
Remove it, use colors, rotate credentials on machines that installed it.

II.Critical .npmrc contains a literal registry token.
Revoke it, reference ${NPM_TOKEN} instead, scrub git history.

III.High No lockfile, so every install may resolve different code.
Run bun install, commit bun.lock, use --frozen-lockfile in CI.

Your army

You declared fourteen packages. They brought three hundred friends. Bun Tsu walks the ranks with a lantern.

  • The squatter waits at the crossroads wearing your friend’s name, one letter missing.Typosquats: names one edit from a popular package.
  • A postinstall script is a guest who arrives carrying his own key.Install scripts that fetch or eval code, and your trustedDependencies.
  • The one who came to you as chalk@5.6.1 wears stolen armour.Exact versions from documented compromises stop the install outright.

Consult the oracle

Ask for a saying about a particular danger, a particular kind of package, or both. 168 sayings, 33 of them Sun Tzu’s own.

Usage

Ask for a prophecy

Reads the project you are in and lists what is wrong and what to do. Ask for the prophecy and the scroll comes first.

bunx @wisdomousai/bun-tsu
bunx @wisdomousai/bun-tsu --prophecy
bunx @wisdomousai/bun-tsu config style prophecy  # always
bunx @wisdomousai/bun-tsu --strict  # fail CI

Post him at the gate

Bun’s security scanner hook runs him before every bun install. He stops versions OSV lists as malware and questions look-alike names and suspicious new releases.

bun add -d @wisdomousai/bun-tsu
bunx bun-tsu init
# bunfig.toml
[install.security]
scanner = "@wisdomousai/bun-tsu"

In Claude Code, the plugin posts him in front of the agent too. Every bun add, npm install or npx it runs is checked first, and each session starts with the project’s findings.

/plugin marketplace add wisdomousai/bun-tsu
/plugin install bun-tsu@bun-tsu

Hear a saying a day

A preload prints one saying, once a day, when you run something interactively. Silent in CI and pipes.

bun add -d @wisdomousai/bun-tsu
bunx bun-tsu init --preload
# bunfig.toml
preload = ["@wisdomousai/bun-tsu/preload"]

Bun Tsu is a joke with a real scanner inside. Malware and advisories come from OSV.dev, release history from the npm registry, and a small hand-checked list of documented compromises covers offline use. Sun Tzu lines are from Lionel Giles’ 1910 translation, in the public domain. Everything else is apocrypha.
